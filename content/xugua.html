序卦 — The Sequence of the Hexagrams

Original text with the translation of James Legge (1882). One of the Ten Wings (十翼) of the YiJing.

有天地，然后万物生焉。盈天地之间者唯万物，故受之以《屯》。

When there were heaven and earth, then afterwards all things were produced. What fills up (the space) between heaven and earth are (those) all things. Hence (Qián and Kūn) are followed by Zhūn.

《屯》者，盈也。屯者，物之始生也。物生必蒙，故受之以《蒙》。

Zhūn denotes filling up. Zhūn is descriptive of things on their first production. When so produced, they are sure to be in an undeveloped condition. Hence Zhūn is followed by Méng.

《蒙》者，蒙也，物之稺也。物稺不可不养也，故受之以《需》。

Méng is descriptive of what is undeveloped,--the young of creatures and things. These in that state require to be nourished. Hence Méng is followed by Xū.

《需》者，饮食之道也。饮食必有讼，故受之以《讼》。

Xū is descriptive of the way in which meat and drink (come to be supplied). Over meat and drink there are sure to be contentions. Hence Xū is followed by Sòng.

讼必有众起，故受之以《师》。

Sòng is sure to cause the rising up of the multitudes; and hence it is followed by Shī.

《师》者，众也。众必有所比，故受之以《比》。

Shī has the signification of multitudes, and between multitudes there must be some bond of union. Hence it is followed by Bǐ.

《比》者，比也。比必有所畜，故受之以《小畜》。

Bǐ denotes being attached to. (Multitudes in) union must be subjected to some restraint. Hence Bǐ is followed by Xiǎo Xù.

物畜然后有礼，故受之以《履》。

When things are subjected to restraint, there come to be rites of ceremony, and hence Xiǎo Xù is followed by Lǚ.

履而泰然后安，故受之以《泰》。

The treading (on what is proper) leads to Tài, which issues in a state of freedom and repose, and hence Lǚ is followed by Tài.

《泰》者，通也。物不可以终通，故受之以《否》。

Tài denotes things having free course. They cannot have that for ever, and hence it is followed by Pǐ (denoting being shut up and restricted).

物不可以终否，故受之以《同人》。

Things cannot for ever be shut up, and hence Pǐ is followed by Tóng Rén.

与人同者，物必归焉，故受之以《大有》。

To him who cultivates union with men, things must come to belong, and hence Tóng Rén is followed by Dà Yǒu.

有大者不可以盈，故受之以《谦》。

Those who, have what is great should not allow in themselves the feeling of being full, and hence Dà Yǒu is followed by Qiān.

有大而能谦必豫，故受之以《豫》。

When great possessions are associated with humility, there is sure to be pleasure and satisfaction; and hence Qiān is followed by Yù.

豫必有随，故受之以《随》。

Where such complacency is awakened, (he who causes it) is sure to have followers (Suí).

以喜随人者必有事，故受之以《蛊》。

They who follow another are sure to have services (to perform), and hence Suí is followed by Gǔ.

《蛊》者，事也。有事而后可大，故受之以《临》。

Gǔ means (the performance of) services. He who performs such services may afterwards become great, and hence Gǔ is followed by Lín.

《临》者，大也。物大然后可观，故受之以《观》。

Lín means great. What is great draws forth contemplation, and hence Lín is followed by Guān.

可观而后有所合，故受之以《噬嗑》。

He who attracts contemplation will then bring about the union of others with himself, and hence Guān is followed by Shì Hé.

嗑者，合也。物不可以苟合而已，故受之以《贲》。

Shì Hé means union. But things should not be united in a reckless or irregular way, and hence Shì Hé is followed by Bì.

《贲》者，饰也。致饰然后亨则尽矣，故受之以《剥》。

Bì denotes adorning. When ornamentation has been carried to the utmost, its progress comes to an end; and hence Bì is followed by Po.

《剥》者，剥也。物不可以终尽剥，穷上反下，故受之以《复》。

Po denotes decay and overthrow. Things cannot be done away for ever. When decadence and overthrow have completed their work at one end, reintegration commences at the other; and hence Po is followed by Fù.

复则不妄矣，故受之以《无妄》。

When the return (thus indicated) has taken place, we have not any rash disorder, and Fù. is followed by Wú Wàng.

有无妄，然后可畜，故受之以《大畜》。

Given the freedom from disorder and insincerity (which this name denotes), there may be the accumulation (of virtue), and Wú Wàng is followed by Dà Xù.

物畜然后可养，故受之以《颐》。

Such accumulation having taken place, there will follow the nourishment of it; and hence Dà Xù is followed by Yí.

《颐》者，养也。不养则不可动，故受之以《大过》。

Yí denotes nourishing. Without nourishment there could be no movement, and hence Yí is followed by Dà Guò.

物不可以终过，故受之以《坎》。

Things cannot for ever be in a state of extraordinary (progress); and hence Dà Guò is followed by Kǎn.

《坎》者，陷也。陷必有所丽，故受之以《离》。

Kǎn denotes falling into peril. When one falls into peril, he is sure to attach himself to some person or thing; and hence Kǎn is followed by Lí.

《离》者，丽也。

Lí denotes being attached, or adhering, to.

有天地然后有万物，有万物然后有男女，有男女然后有夫妇，有夫妇然后有父子，有父子然后有君臣，有君臣然后有上下，有上下然后礼义有所错。

Heaven and earth existing, all (material) things then got their existence. All (material) things having existence, afterwards there came male and female. From the existence of male and female there came afterwards husband and wife. From husband and wife there came father and son. From father and son there came ruler and minister. From ruler and minister there came high and low. When (the distinction of) high and low had existence, afterwards came the arrangements of propriety and righteousness.

夫妇之道不可以不久也，故受之以《恒》。

The rule for the relation of husband and wife is that it should be long-enduring. Hence Xián is followed by Héng.

《恒》者，久也。物不可以久居其所，故受之以《遯》。

Héng denotes long enduring. Things cannot long abide in the same place; and hence Héng is followed by Dùn.

《遯》者，退也。物不可以终遯，故受之以《大壮》。

Dùn denotes withdrawing. Things cannot be for ever withdrawn; and hence Dùn is succeeded by Dà Zhuàng.

物不可以终壮，故受之以《晋》。

Things cannot remain forever (simply) in the state of vigour; and hence Dà Zhuàng is succeeded by Jìn.

《晋》者，进也。进必有所伤，故受之以《明夷》。

Jìn denotes advancing. (But) advancing is sure to lead to being wounded; and hence Jìn is succeeded by Míng Yí.

夷者，伤也。伤于外者必反于家，故受之以《家人》。

Yí denotes being wounded. He who is wounded abroad will return to his home; and hence Míng Yí is followed by Jiā Rén.

家道穷必乖，故受之以《睽》。

When the right administration of the family is at an end, misunderstanding and division will ensue; and hence Jiā Rén is followed by Kuí.

《睽》者，乖也。乖必有难，故受之以《蹇》。

Kuí denotes misunderstanding and division; and such a state is sure to give rise to difficulties and complications. Kuí therefore is followed by Jiǎn.

《蹇》者，难也。物不可以终难，故受之以《解》。

Jiǎn denotes difficulties; but things cannot remain for ever in such a state. Jiǎn therefore is followed by Xiè.

《解》者，缓也。缓必有所失，故受之以《损》。

Xiè denotes relaxation and ease. In a state of relaxation and ease there are sure to be losses; and hence Xiè is followed by Sǔn.

损而不已必益，故受之以《益》。

But when Sǔn (or diminution) is going on without end, increase is sure to come. Sǔn therefore is followed by Yì.

益而不已必决，故受之以《夬》。

When increase goes on without end, there is sure to come a dispersing of it, and hence Yì is followed by Guài.

《夬》者，决也。决必有遇，故受之以《姤》。

Guài denotes dispersion. But dispersion must be succeeded by a meeting (again). Hence Guài is followed by Gòu.

《姤》者，遇也。物相遇而后聚，故受之以《萃》。

Gòu denotes such meeting. When things meet together, a collection is then formed. Hence Gòu is followed by Cuì.

《萃》者，聚也。聚而上者谓之升，故受之以《升》。

Cuì denotes being collected. When (good men) are collected and mount to the highest places, there results what we call an upward advance; and hence Cuì is followed by Shēng.

升而不已必困，故受之以《困》。

When such advance continues without stopping, there is sure to come distress; and hence Shēng is followed by Kùn.

困乎上者必反下，故受之以《井》。

When distress is felt in the height (that has been gained), there is sure to be a return to the ground beneath; and hence Kùn is followed by Jǐng.

井道不可不革，故受之以《革》。

What happens under Jǐng requires to be changed, and hence it is followed by Gé (denoting change).

革物者莫若鼎，故受之以《鼎》。

For changing the substance of things there is nothing equal to the caldron; and hence Gé is followed by Dǐng.

主器者莫若长子，故受之以《震》。

For presiding over (that and all other) vessels, no one is equal to the eldest son, and hence Dǐng is followed by Zhèn.

《震》者，动也。物不可以终动，止之，故受之以《艮》。

Zhèn conveys the idea of putting in motion. But things cannot be kept in motion forever. The motion is stopped; and hence Zhèn is followed by Gèn.

《艮》者，止也。物不可以终止，故受之以《渐》。

Gèn gives the idea of arresting or stopping. Things cannot be kept for ever in a state of repression, and hence Gèn is followed by Jiàn.

渐者，进也。进必有所归，故受之以《归妹》。

Jiàn gives the idea of (gradually) advancing. With advance there must be a certain point that is arrived at, and hence Jiàn is succeeded by Guī Mèi.

得其所归者必大，故受之以《丰》。

When things thus find the proper point to which to come, they are sure to become great. Hence Guī Mèi is succeeded by Fēng.

《丰》者，大也。穷大者必失其居，故受之以《旅》。

Fēng conveys the idea of being great. He whose greatness reaches the utmost possibility, is sure to lose his dwelling; and hence Fēng is succeeded by Lǚ (denoting travellers or strangers).

旅而无所容，故受之以《巽》。

We have in it the idea of strangers who have no place to receive them, and hence Lǚ is followed by Xùn.

《巽》者，入也。入而后说之，故受之以《兑》。

Xùn gives the idea of (penetrating and) entering. One enters (on the pursuit of his object), and afterwards has pleasure in it; hence Xùn is followed by Duì.

《兑》者，说也。说而后散之，故受之以《涣》。

Duì denotes pleasure and satisfaction. This pleasure and satisfaction (begins) afterwards to be dissipated, and hence Duì is followed by Huàn.

《涣》者，离也。物不可以终离，故受之以《节》。

Huàn denotes separation and division. A state of division cannot continue for ever, and therefore Huàn is followed by Jié.

节而信之，故受之以《中孚》。

Jié (or the system of regulations) having been established, men believe in it, and hence it is followed by Zhōng Fú.

有其信者必行之，故受之以《小过》。

When men have the belief which Zhōng Fú implies, they are sure to carry it into practice; and hence it is succeeded by Xiǎo Guò.

有过物者必济，故受之以《既济》。

He that surpasses others is sure to remedy (evils that exist), and therefore Xiǎo Guò is succeeded by Jì Jì.

物不可穷也，故受之以《未济》，终焉。

But the succession of events cannot come to an end, and therefore Jì Jì is succeeded by Wèi Jì, with which (the hexagrams) come to a close.
