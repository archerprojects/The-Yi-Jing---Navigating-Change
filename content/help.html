User Guide 易經

Getting Started — For first time users

Select + New User to establish an alias and create a resident profile that will store journal entries of your casting history. Guests can enter without creating a profile, but those readings are not saved to a journal. To start a reading, use the ◀ ▶ arrows under Who is consulting? on the opening screen to pick a saved user or Guest. Filling in your profile (Journal → Profile) — date and time of birth, birth location, current location — gives each reading a richer, more personal frame: those details are captured and shown beside the reading in your journal. NO INFORMATION IS SHARED OR SENT OUTSIDE THE APP.

The Questions

Before the oracle opens you answer four things: your Question, a Purpose (pick any that fit), your Mood (pick one), and the day's Weather/conditions (pick any). The Proceed to the Oracle button stays locked until a user or Guest is selected and all four are answered — the casting controls are held until you've settled the question you're bringing. When the sixth line is cast, a journal entry records the reading and the moment.

Casting Methods

  • Three coins — the quick, familiar method; each throw of three coins gives one line, building the hexagram from the bottom up.
  • Yarrow Stalks — the older, slower ritual division, animated stalk by stalk; its line probabilities differ from the coins, as tradition holds.
  • Manual Cast Entry (Journal menu) — record a reading you cast by hand, away from the app.

Switch methods freely before the first line; switching is blocked mid-cast (you'll be asked to finish first); once a reading is complete, choosing a method begins a new one.

Reading the Result

The hexagram assembles line by line; any changing lines produce the relating hexagram. Read it across five sources — Wilhelm, Legge, Hatcher, 中文, and the Ten Wings / 十翼.

Study

The Study menu opens the texts to browse on their own.

The Hexagram Wheel sets all 64 hexagrams in a ring, with three ways to open one:

  • Drag — click the wheel and drag to spin it; release and it slows, settles on a hexagram, and opens it.
  • Spin button — click ↻ Spin the Wheel to spin it at random; it slows, settles on a hexagram, and opens it.
  • Click — click any hexagram on the wheel to open it directly.

Hovering over a hexagram shows its number, character, and name in the side panel.

Alongside the Wheel, Study holds the Ten Wings treatises, each on its own page — 繫辭上 / 繫辭下 (the Great Treatise, I and II), 說卦 (Discussion of the Trigrams), 序卦 (the Sequence of the Hexagrams), and 雜卦 (the Hexagrams in Miscellaneous order) — and Trigram Lookup, where you choose an upper and a lower trigram and it identifies and opens the hexagram they form.

Weighting

Weighting goes a step further in personalising the reading. It factors in the time of day, the season, the phase of the moon, your light, your sleep and your own daily and monthly rhythms, to give a more precise reflection of your state of mind and body at the time of the reading. It is new and experimental, and offered openly as this app's own design.

With weighting off, the cast follows the classical odds exactly: yarrow stalks 1, 5, 7 and 3 in 16; coins 1, 3, 3 and 1 in 8.

Turning it on. On the opening page the Weighting switch sits under Who is consulting?, with a (?) beside it. It starts Off for every reading. Its entries stay greyed until it is on:

  • Strength — Subtle, Moderate or Strong, under the switch: how far the odds may move.
  • Slept — hours of sleep last night; Woke at — the time you woke today. They sit under Purpose, and both are needed when weighting is on.
  • Light today… — about how many minutes of each kind of light you have had, and when (below). It starts from your light habits, so on a typical day there is nothing to change.
  • Cycle day — shown only if cycle tracking is on in your profile; Started today resets it.
  • Now — beside Proceed, worked out for you: time of day, moon phase, season and solar term, today's daylight and the lunar tide.

Facts that rarely change are set once in Journal → Profile: date of birth (your age bracket), sex, current location (for day length and the season's hemisphere), daytime sun, evening light, regularity of sleep times, and cycle tracking (optional). A profile user needs sex set to use weighting.

How it works. Two things move the odds of the four line types:

  • Direction — the cycle of yin and yang. The four line types are the four stages of one cycle: young yang (7) growing, old yang (9) at its height, young yin (8) turning, old yin (6) at rest. The moment's place on that cycle leans the odds toward its stage.
  • Amount of change — your state. Your mood and the research-weighted inputs below raise or lower the chance of moving lines (6 and 9). The further today is from a rested, well-lit day, the more lines are likely to move.

Each line type's odds can move at most 15% (Subtle), 30% (Moderate) or 50% (Strong) of its classical value, and every line type keeps at least half its classical chance. The cast itself stays real: each coin shows its face, and the yarrow stalks are divided into real piles that give the drawn line.

The cycle of yin and yang.

  • Season and solar term. Zhu Xi matched the four stages to the seasons: 春为少阳，夏为老阳，秋为少阴，冬为老阴 — spring young yang, summer old yang, autumn young yin, winter old yin (Zhuzi Yulei 137). Each stage is centred on its equinox or solstice, found from the sun's position along its path (the 24 solar terms). South of the equator the seasons are reversed.
  • Time of day. The day as an analogy to Zhu Xi's seasons: dawn young yang, noon old yang, dusk young yin, midnight old yin, changing gradually. This analogy is our own.
  • Moon phase. The Cantong qi (周易參同契, chapter 18) follows the moon through the trigrams: Zhèn on the third day, Duì on the eighth, Qián complete at the full moon, then Xùn, Gèn at the twenty-third and Kūn at the dark of the moon. So: dark moon old yin, waxing young yang, full moon old yang, waning young yin.

Light: when and what kind. What light does depends on when it falls and what kind it is. Light before the body's nightly low point delays the body clock; light after it advances it (Khalsa et al. 2003), so morning daylight and late-evening light work in opposite directions. Blue-rich light — daylight, screens, cool LEDs — sets the clock and suppresses melatonin most (Brainard et al. 2001; Thapan et al. 2001); warm, dim light does far less; red and near-infrared work through a different channel. Light today… records minutes of daylight, bright or screen light, warm or dim light, and red or near-infrared therapy across morning, midday, afternoon, evening and night of your own day. The weather you chose scales the daylight.

Factor Weighting. Journal → Factor Weighting… shows every weight and limit with its evidence. The table is locked; unlock it to change a value, and Reset to Defaults restores the built-in table. Changes are kept per profile. These are first settings, to be refined with further research.

What it records. Each journal entry notes whether weighting was on, its strength, every input, the table it was cast with and the odds that resulted, so weighted and classical readings can be compared. A Weighted marker beside your name on the casting panel links back here.

The Journal

For profile users every completed reading is saved automatically — your question, purpose, mood and weather; the auto-captured moment (moon phase, date, time, location); and your profile context. In View Journal you can filter and search your entries, select one to see its full detail with the hexagram text in zoomable Wilhelm / Legge / Hatcher panels, and right-click any entry to Read it in the reader, Edit it, or Delete it. Below each entry's detail, Save Reflection & Tags adds your later thoughts to it. Profile lets you edit your details — and from a Guest session, create a profile; the reading you just cast is carried into it as its first entry. Finished readings export with Export to PDF.

Keyboard Shortcuts

Ctrl+N — new reading · Ctrl+S — export to PDF · Ctrl+B or Esc — back to your reading · Ctrl+Q — quit.

Privacy

Everything — profiles, journals, and readings — is stored only on your own machine. No information is shared or sent outside the app.
